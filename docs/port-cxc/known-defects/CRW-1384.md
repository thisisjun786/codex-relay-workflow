# CRW-1384 — five session-engine candidates of the CRW-1331 review: an interrupt intent that outlived its turn, a repair that lost its answer, a held repair that named the task twice, a checkpoint that dropped earlier links, a start commit of an earlier allocation

Source: CRW's own design (CRW-1316 design sections 3, 5 and 6.3), not the CXC oracle (CXC v0.2.40, commit 3c1459ac): the oracle has no flow engine. The candidates are the confirmed P1s of a V5 review of CRW-1331's pre-fix head `710f4909`, and in the B1 and A4+B4 seams the two the V5 review of this issue's head `66051878` confirmed and the two of `268449ee`; each was reproduced first with a test that fails when the candidate is real, the five on `origin/dev` `7d2430bb` (after CRW-1362+1367 and CRW-1278+1327), the two on `66051878`, the two on `268449ee`. The Go side is `internal/flow/engine` (`session.go`: `sessionIntent`, `sessionBindStopped` and `conversationTurn` new, `sessionStageOf`, `sessionRecover`, `sessionTurn`, `sessionBind`, `sessionVerify`, `sessionAdopt`, `sessionStop`, `sessionResumeHeld`, `startCommit`; `journal.go`: `journalFlush` new; `call.go`: `interrupted`, `threadPrefix`, `conversationPrefix`, `callConv` and `callConversation` new, `threadTurns`; `checkpoint.go`: `checkpointPacket`). The tests are in `internal/flow/engine/session_e2a_test.go`.

- B1: an engine interrupt the previous process asked for (`interrupt_requested`) was a reason of the call rather than of a turn, so a resumed process applied it to turns it was never asked for: the next attempt's turn after the interrupted attempt was lost (before the restart or after it), the turn of a spawn step no subagent was bound to when it came, and the turn the host started for a repair step (`send_input`) the owner had not taken when it came - a step the resumed process publishes again, or the file the dead process left; the replacement's completed turn was held `engine_paused` "after the engine interrupted it"; port: fixed (one rule: an interrupt goes with the turn it was asked for, `sessionIntent`, the step's `op` its row names, the attempt and the subagent and turn the rows bound to that step, and `interrupted` compares it with the turn the call is on where the outcome is read, so every other turn starts with no intent and nothing clears it on a path. A stop binds the turn that the subagent of a step not bound yet (a `send_input`, an adopted one) started for it before it interrupts it (`sessionBindStopped`), so the rows name the turn the interrupt reaches. That `turn_started`, written after the `interrupt_requested` intent, waited in the open batch of the SQLite journal while the interrupt was sent, so a process that died then lost it and the resumed process read the repair turn the interrupt reached as interrupted by someone else (`host_interrupted_external`); the stop now flushes the journal before it sends the interrupt (`sessionStop`), so the turn is stored when the interrupt reaches it. Pinned by the three cases of `TestSessionInterruptIntentStaysWithTheTurnItInterrupted` and the four of `TestSessionInterruptIntentDecidesOnlyItsTurn`: the repair step the owner had not taken and the one it takes after the restart complete, the repair turn the stop interrupted and the bound turn that ends after the restart are held `engine_paused`; and by `TestSessionStopBindIsStoredBeforeTheInterrupt`: a process that dies right after its stop interrupted the repair turn leaves that turn held `engine_paused` over the memory and the SQLite journal).
- B2: a repair turn sent to the open subagent (`send_input`) recorded no `previous_answer`, and a resume from its bound or lost stage did not restore the answer, so the spawn of the repair turn's next attempt after a restart had no "## Your previous answer"; port: fixed (`step_published` of a repair turn records `previous_answer` whatever its action, an added member of the row that keeps the store's schema, and `sessionRecover` restores it at the bound and the lost stage. Pinned by the two cases of `TestSessionRepairKeepsThePreviousAnswerAcrossARestart`).
- B2, second half (a `send_input` step looked up on the host neither at its `step_published` recovery nor while it waits): not reproduced, CRW-1367 fixed it before this lane (`sessionFind` has a `StepSendInput` case, which `sessionTaken` calls for a recovered step and the find ticker of `sessionBind` every sixth poll; `sessionVerify` binds the marker turn on the subagent the input was for). The cases `repair sent before the report` and `repair turn_started` of `TestSessionRunDeathAtEachRow`, which count the repair inputs the owner was sent (one), pass on the base: `--- PASS: TestSessionRunDeathAtEachRow/memory/repair_sent_before_the_report`, `.../sqlite/repair_sent_before_the_report`, `.../memory/repair_turn_started`, `.../sqlite/repair_turn_started`.
- B3: a held session call whose repair turn was delivered went on with a new subagent at `firstK` 1 (the call's one repair is used) and with the original task as the turn's input, so the packet named the task under "## Task" and again under "## What to do now", with no answer to repair; port: fixed (the turn's input is the held repair turn's own instruction and the answer it repaired comes from that step's `previous_answer`; the call still has no second repair, as `resumeHeld` counts it on the App Server path. Pinned by `TestSessionHeldRepairGoesOnAsTheRepair`).
- B3, the rest: not reproduced. That the new subagent gets no repair turn of its own is the one-repair rule of every call (`hold.go` `resumeHeld` sets `firstK` 1 for a held call whose repair was used), not a defect. The `stagePublished` branch of `sessionResumeHeld` that leaves `s.agent` unset is never reached with a `send_input` step: a `send_input` step follows a turn that was bound, whose `turn_started` sets `delivered` (`sessionVerify`, `sessionRestore`), so the held row says `input_state: delivered` and the call takes the first branch.
- A4+B4: the checkpoint packet of a session continuation inside a suffix read `threadPrefix`, which inherits a thread's earlier turns only on a fork: a chained continuation (c3 of c2 of c1) recorded only c2's turns, and a continuation of a call whose repair finished on a second subagent recorded the repair turn without the task turn; and a call held after a delivered turn, which goes on in place on a new subagent at the next k, had its held turn recorded beside the turn that replaced it, so the task (held at k 1, gone on with at k 2) or the repair (held at k 2, gone on with at k 3) was in the packet twice; and a turn's place in the conversation was reconstructed from the held rows and k, so a call held before any subagent took its first step (`input_state: not_delivered`), which goes on with that step in place without a `step_published` row of its own, had its repair turn read as a held call's turn gone on with in place and put over the task turn: when the repair finished on another subagent (a lost repair attempt run again, a repair held and gone on with in place) the packet held the repair and no task; port: fixed (one invariant: a turn's place in its call's conversation is recorded, not reconstructed. `step_published` and the `turn_started` of its turn record `conversation_turn`, the conversation turn the turn is - the call's task turn 1, its one repair 2, on whatever subagent, attempt and k it runs (`conversationTurn`) - and, for a turn run again - the next attempt of a lost turn, the turn a held call goes on with in place - `replaces_turn`, the turn the conversation held there (added members of the rows, no store schema change). `conversationPrefix` reads each session call's conversation from those rows alone (`callConv`): each turn at the conversation turn its row records, each conversation turn once, a turn that names the turn it replaces in that turn's place; it gives that list with each turn's input, from which alone `checkpointPacket` is built, and for a session subagent it adds the conversation of the handle its call continues and the call's conversation before the turn its subagent started at. A `turn_started` row of a journal written before the place was recorded (no `conversation_turn`) takes it from the `repair` member of its step's `step_published` row (the task turn 1, the repair 2; every `step_published` row has the member), a later turn at that place replacing the earlier one, so an older journal is read from recorded members too: the reconstruction from held rows and k is gone, and an older journal of the shape above reads the task and then the repair. Pinned by `TestSessionCheckpointHoldsTheWholeConversation`: its third case holds a turn the next attempt ran again once, its fourth and fifth a call held in its task turn and in its repair turn and gone on with in place; by `TestSessionConversationTurnIsRecorded`: a call held before its first step was taken, its repair finished on the task turn's subagent, on a new subagent after a lost attempt and in place after a hold, holds the task and then the repair once each, and its rows record conversation turn 1 for the task turn, 2 for every repair turn and the turn a repair run again replaces; and by `TestSessionConversationOfAJournalWithoutRecordedTurns`: six journals whose rows record no place - a call held before its first step was taken whose repair was lost and run again or held and gone on with in place, a task turn and a repair turn held and gone on with in place, a repair finished on a second subagent, a first turn lost and run again - read the task and the repair once each from their steps' `repair` member).
- A4+B4, the digest chain (`threadChain` in the input digest of a continuation): not changed. The replay rule replays a position only after every position before it on its path, so a continuation's earlier links are proven by their own positions: when c1 runs again, c2 (whose own chain holds c1's turn) is not replayed and runs again with new turn ids, which changes c3's chain. Changing the chain would change the input digests that existing session runs recorded and make their resumes run those positions again. The code path: `resume.go` `resumePrior` replays a call only while its path is not live, and `live` (`walk.go` `markLive`) makes every later position of the path a suffix position, so a continuation after a link that ran again is never replayed. `TestSessionContinuationAfterRestart` and the chain case of `TestSessionCheckpointHoldsTheWholeConversation` replay their first calls with the digests as before.
- A5: `startCommit` took the start from a fresh or restore allocation row of the call's worktree only when no node `crw.commit` row had set it, so a worktree restored at another commit than an earlier allocation's `crw.commit` (a superseded process's, past a suffix's boundary) was judged against that commit: `executor_head_moved` was recorded for a HEAD that had not moved, and `crw.commit` (`internal/flow/ops/lane_build.go`) would have committed the pending tree on the earlier commit; port: fixed (the allocation row of the call's worktree resets the start to its `start_commit`, and only a `crw.commit` after it moves it. Pinned by `TestSessionStartCommitIsTheAllocationsStart`).
