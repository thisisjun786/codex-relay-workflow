# CRW-1210 — the Stop hook wakes the daemon; the send budget counts events and has an orchestrator class

Source: CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac) `policy.py` `RetryPolicy` and `pacing` (the hour cap and its count), `daemon.py` `run` (the fixed-interval loop) and `control.py` `GuardServer` (the control socket that answers the Stop hook). The Go side is `internal/relay/delivery/policy.go` (`RetryPolicy`, `HourlyCapFor`, `PacingAt`), `internal/relay/store/sendbudget.go` (`RelationshipSendsSQL`, `RelationshipSpentSQL`, `OrchestratorParentSQL`), `internal/relay/daemon/{wake,budgets,daemon,census,observe}.go`, `internal/relay/hook/adapter.go` (`HandleControlWake`), `internal/relay/service/control.go` (`ListenControlWake`) and `internal/relay/cli/daemon.go`. The design is the 0.5.0-alpha design issue R02 (R1 and R8); `docs/relay/invariants.md` I-475 and `docs/relay/operations.md` are rewritten in the same change.

- The daemon ticked only every `PollInterval` (20 s), so a managed child's completion waited up to 20 s for its observation and delivery, though the Stop hook that reports the end of the turn reaches the same process over the control socket the moment it happens (`daemon.py` run; `control.py` GuardServer); port: fixed (`hook.HandleControlWake` tells an in-process wake the child's thread when the guard selected an assignment and released the Stop; `daemon.RunWoken` ends its wait on it and runs a tick at once, never less than `MinWakeGap` (1 s) after the last tick began, so the per-tick budgets hold under a storm of Stops; the woken threads' turns are read first by `observe`; the 20 s tick stays as the backstop; no socket method and no schema change; pinned by `TestWakeRunsTickWithoutPollInterval`, `TestWakeStormDebounced`, `TestWokenThreadObservedFirst` and `TestTickBackstopStillRuns` in `internal/relay/daemon/wake_test.go`, `TestThroughputWokenTickKeepsTheReadBudget` in `internal/relay/daemon/throughput_test.go` (a woken tick keeps the read budget), and `TestWakeFromManagedChildStop`, `TestWakeNotSentForAHeldStop`, `TestWakeNotSentForAnUnmanagedStop`, `TestWakeIsOptionalForHandleControl` and the end-to-end `TestWakeRunsTickWithoutPollInterval` in `internal/relay/hook/wake_test.go`).
- The hour cap counted attempts, so one event that was tried again (an attempt in flight that is retried, a redelivery after a lost turn) spent several of the relationship's twelve sends and a calm relationship could read as a runaway (the `hourly_cap` false positive the real-environment trial is to measure, F17) (`policy.py` `pacing`, the count of `attempts` rows); port: fixed (the count is `COUNT(DISTINCT event_id)` for delivery attempts and `COUNT(DISTINCT message_id)` for supervisor transports, in `RelationshipSendsSQL`, `RelationshipSpentSQL` and everything that reads them; what bounds the repetition of one event is its own attempt cap, `MaxAttempts` held at `attempt_cap` and `BusyMaxAttempts` at `busy_cap`, which are unchanged; pinned by `TestPacingCountsDistinctEvents` and `TestPerEventAttemptCapKept` in `internal/relay/delivery/policy_test.go`, with the store's `TestRelationshipSends_counts_one_relationship_to_one_recipient_in_one_window` and the readers' `TestSendBudget_the_readers_agree_on_a_relationships_own_hour` changed to count events). The test seed `spendHour` in the delivery harness now seeds `n` events with one attempt each (it seeded one event with `n` attempts), so the goldens that print those seeded rows changed.
- Every relationship had the one cap of twelve an hour, though an orchestrator (the parent of a project) receives the results of every issue its project runs (`policy.py` `RetryPolicy.max_sends_per_relationship_per_hour`); port: fixed (a relationship whose parent holds the live parent binding of a project, active or paused and not replaced, is of class orchestrator and has `MaxSendsPerOrchestratorRelationshipPerHour` (60, the starting value; F17 measures the real one) in `HourlyCapFor`, the eligibility and busy-head reads, `status`'s `sendPacing` and `assignment-show`'s copy of the rule; the class is read from the binding, so no schema or column is added; pinned by `TestOrchestratorClassBudget` (the claim, the eligibility and busy-head reads), `TestPacingReadsTheCapOfTheRelationshipsClass` (`assignment-show`'s copy) and `TestSendBudget_status_reads_an_orchestrators_own_cap` (`status`); a test that sets the ordinary cap on a relationship whose parent owns a project must set the orchestrator cap too, as `Test259_a_supervisor_send_is_charged_to_its_relationship_and_recipient` now does).
- The per-tick budgets (`MaxProjects`, `MaxSupervisorSends`, `MaxSendsPerParentPerTick`, with the tick's `MaxSends`) were constants chosen for one project, so a daemon serving several orchestrators visited too few projects and sent too few reports a tick; port: fixed (`Policy.BudgetsFor` raises them by what each live orchestrator needs, counted from `scope_bindings` each tick and bounded by `MaxLiveOrchestrators` (32); with none live the constants stand; pinned by `TestTickBudgetsScaleWithOrchestrators`). The Linear text names `MaxSendsPerParentPerTick` as computed from the live orchestrator count; the formula chosen (three attempts for each orchestrator, and three for a parent while any is live) is a starting value like the cap above.
- Deviation, recorded: the Linear criterion says `MaxSendsPerRelationshipPerHour` and `MaxAttempts` are "counted as distinct events"; the port reads `MaxAttempts` as the per-event attempt cap the same criterion says stays, so it keeps its meaning (attempts of one event) and only the hour cap changed its unit.
- Verification fix round 1: the hour cap refused the retry of an event the hour already counted (it added no distinct event, but `spent >= cap` was read without asking whether the event was one of the counted), in the due read, the busy-head read, the preflight and the claim; port: fixed (`store.EventCountedSQL` and `MessageCountedSQL` say a subject is already counted, `delivery.SendRefusalFor` and `ReserveSendFor` carry the event or supervisor message, `PacingAt` does not apply the cap to a counted subject, a cap of zero still refuses everything, and the gap and the attempt cap still hold; pinned by `TestRetryOfCountedEventAtCap` in `internal/relay/delivery/policy_test.go` and `TestRetryOfCountedMessageAtCap` in `internal/relay/supervisor/send_budget_test.go`). The woken-first sort in `observe` also pushed the reserved first read of the other observation class behind the woken turns; fixed (the first read of each class stays within the first two reads, which is also where the time-limit exemption sits; pinned by `TestThroughputWokenTickKeepsBothClassesRead`).
- Verification fix round 2: the due read and the busy-head read let the retry of a counted event through the hour cap even when that cap was zero, which `PacingAt` refuses, so a counted retry a cap of zero refuses stayed due, took idle wakes and held its recipient's busy line against the deliveries of another relationship class (an orchestrator's completion behind an ordinary correction to the same recipient); fixed (both reads compare the class cap with zero for a counted event and with the relationship's spent count otherwise, `cap > CASE WHEN counted THEN 0 ELSE spent END`, which is `PacingAt`'s rule with no new argument; pinned by `TestZeroCapExcludesCountedRetry` and `TestZeroCapCountedHeadDoesNotBlockAnotherClass` in `internal/relay/delivery/policy_test.go`). The woken-first order in `observe` moved every woken turn ahead of the rest, so when the same children woke tick after tick and their turns filled the read budget, the turns of the same class nobody woke were never read (I-66); fixed (the woken turns take at most the reads past the pass's floor, `MaxTurnReads` less one read, or two when both classes wait, and the rest of the budget reads the unwoken order from its front, so the longest-waiting rotation goes on; with no read past the floor the order is the unwoken one; pinned by `TestThroughputRepeatedWakesDoNotStarveTheOthers` in `internal/relay/daemon/throughput_test.go`).
