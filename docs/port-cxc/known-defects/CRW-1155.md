# CRW-1155 — a background job runs only under its record, and cancel says cancelled only for a job it saw stop

Source: CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac) `plugins/codexclaw/components/bg-wake/src/spawn.ts:26-32` (newId), `:114-145` (runBackground: spawn, then the record) and `:150-176` (cancel), `registry.ts:111-148` (reconcile). The Go files are `internal/relay/job/spawn.go` (reserve, runBackground with its launch gate, cancel), `internal/relay/job/registry.go` (StatusCancelRequested, reconcile and settle), `internal/relay/job/lock.go` (the store lock and update) and `internal/relay/job/verbs.go` (cliCancel). The tests are in `internal/relay/job/spawn_owner_test.go`; the red run against the code before the change is `/state/crw/crw-lane-bg-job/evidence/red-1155/`. Each item is a deviation from the oracle under the security and record-loss classes of the parity rule.

- Supersedes known-defects.md, section "Found by the bg spawn port", item beginning "A job whose record was never written keeps running unmanaged": now port: fixed (the record is reserved under its id before anything starts, and the shell waits at a launch gate, `read` on descriptor 3, until the launcher has published its pid and start token and writes `go`; a record that cannot be published, a start identity that cannot be read and a reservation a cancel withdrew end the shell at the gate, and a caller that dies before it publishes closes the pipe, so the shell ends without running the command. Pinned by `TestRunBackgroundLeavesNoUntrackedJobWhenThePublishFails`, `TestRunBackgroundLeavesNoUntrackedJobWhenTheCallerDies` and `TestRunBackgroundRefusesAJobWithoutAStartIdentity`).
- Supersedes known-defects.md, section "Found by the bg spawn port", item beginning "`processStartToken` is read once, right after the shell has started": now port: fixed (a shell whose start token cannot be read is not run: `ErrNoStartIdentity`, the record is settled failed with the detail `start identity unreadable`; `cancel` no longer skips the owner check for a record without a token).
- Supersedes known-defects.md, section "Found by the bg spawn port", item beginning "`cancel` signals the group of the pid in a record whose startToken is null": now port: fixed (a pid outside 1 < pid <= MaxInt32, a record without a start token and a token that no longer matches are refused with `ErrOwnerUnproven` and nothing is signalled; the verb exits 1 with the record's status and says how to check the pid by hand, after which the next `get` settles the record. The token is checked again under the store lock, right before the record is changed and the signal follows, so a wait for the lock cannot carry a proof over to a recycled pid; the pid can still be recycled between that check and the signal, a window of one `ps` call).
- Supersedes known-defects.md, section "Found by the bg spawn port", item beginning "`cancel` writes cancelled whether or not a signal landed": now port: fixed (cancel records `cancellation-requested` before it signals the job's process group, returns a failed signal as a `SignalError` with the request kept, and records `cancelled` only once signal 0 to the group answers ESRCH, so a leader that ended while its descendants live is not taken for the job's end. It waits up to two seconds; a group still alive keeps the request, which `Reconcile` settles cancelled once the group has gone, and a second cancel sends SIGKILL to the group, also after the shell itself has ended on SIGTERM, but only for a group the record proves is the job's (the next item). The leader-only fallback after a failed group signal is gone: the group is the job, and a failure to signal it is reported, not retried on one process. Pinned by `TestCancelNeverSignalsAProcessItCannotProveIsTheJob`, `TestCancelReportsASignalThatFailed`, `TestCancelOfAJobThatIgnoresSIGTERMIsNotCancelledUntilItStops`, `TestCancelSaysCancelledOnlyOnceTheWholeGroupIsGone` and `TestCLICancelReportsTheRequestAndTheRefusal`; the replayed `cli__bg__cancel_running_job_kills_group` keeps its output, its two ledger rows and its three `ps` calls).
- Supersedes known-defects.md, section "Found by the bg spawn port", item beginning "The paths of `<id>.out` and `<id>.exit` are cleared before the start", its second half ("`recordExists` and the publication of the record are not one step"): now port: fixed (the id is drawn and the first record published in one step, a temporary file linked to `<id>.json`, which fails when the name exists; pinned by `TestRunBackgroundReservesTheIDBeforeTheStart`). The link half of that item stays with CRW-475.
- A record whose cancel was requested is not terminal: `IsTerminal` is unchanged, so the wake does not deliver it and `list` shows it without a delivery mark until it is settled; an agent waits for `cancelled` (or the job's own outcome). The oracle had no such state; port: fixed (recorded deviation).
- The lifecycle writes of a workspace (the start's publication, reconcile, cancel) take an exclusive `flock` on the bg directory and re-read the record before they write it, so one writer never puts back a snapshot another has changed (`update` in `lock.go`); no lock file is created. The oracle had no lock; port: fixed (recorded deviation, shared with CRW-1092).
- A cancel that read a reservation (no pid) and finds the pid published when it takes the store lock acts on the started job: it re-reads the record and cancels it with the same ownership proof, instead of returning the unchanged running record without an error (`TestCancelOfAReservationWhoseLaunchPublishesItsPidActsOnTheStartedJob`).
- A second cancel after the shell has ended signals the group only when it is shown to be the job's, not because its leader is absent and a group of that number answers signal 0: a group id is handed out again once the group is empty, so that proves nothing of whose group it is, and the first version of this fallback could SIGKILL the members of an unrelated group that took the number. The first cancel writes the moment it showed the shell alive (its start token matched, under the store lock) into the record as `cancelRequestedAt`, a key the oracle's record does not have (kept and written back like any key the port does not name, and dropped when the job is over, so a finished record has the oracle's thirteen keys again; `TestTheCancelRequestMomentLeavesWithTheJob`, and the replayed `cli__bg__cancel_running_job_kills_group` keeps its records). A later cancel whose leader token does not match lists the host's processes (`ps -A -o pid=,ppid=,pgid=,lstart=`) and signals only when every process of the group started no later than the second of that moment, or descends from one that did (`groupProven` in `group.go`); a group that took the number later was formed after the job's group had emptied, and cancel saw the job's group alive for `cancelWait` after that moment, so its members start later. A record without the key (an old one, or one edited by hand), a member whose start does not read, a process list that fails to read and a group with a member nothing connects to the job are refused with `ErrOwnerUnproven` and nothing is signalled, as for any unproven pid. The cost: a descendant that a surviving member started and that was reparented before it was seen, within the second of the request, is not recognised; the operator checks by hand. Pinned by `TestCancelOfARequestedJobSignalsOnlyAGroupItCanShowIsTheJobs`, `TestCancelChecksTheOwnerAgainAfterWaitingForTheStoreLock` and `TestGroupProven`; port: fixed (recorded deviation).
- The record of a job being launched is a reservation until its pid is published, marked `"launching": true` (not one of the oracle's thirteen keys; it leaves the record with the pid, the failure or the cancel). The id's stale files (`<id>.exit`, `.exit.tmp`, `.out`) are cleared after the reservation is visible, so a reader that reconciled it could take the previous job's exit file for this job's result and settle it, and the launch would then be refused as `ErrLaunchWithdrawn`. A reservation is judged by its age alone and never from an exit file, in `Reconcile`, in the re-read of the exit file under the lock and in every reader (`list`, `get`, the hooks); a record without the mark keeps the oracle's rule (the exit file answers first). Pinned by `TestAReservationIsNotSettledFromAnExitFile` and `TestTheReservationMarkLeavesWithTheReservation`; port: fixed (recorded deviation).
