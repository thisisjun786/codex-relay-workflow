# CRW-1409 — a stop bound a step already bound to a turn again

Source: CRW's own design (CRW-1316 design sections 3 and 5), not the CXC oracle (CXC v0.2.40, commit 3c1459ac): the oracle has no flow engine. The item is the P3 the V5 review of the CRW-1405 lane (head `2c214969`) confirmed. The Go side is `internal/flow/engine/session.go` (`sessionStopBinds`). The test is `TestSessionStopNeverBindsABoundStepAgain` (`internal/flow/engine/session_1409_test.go`).

- `sessionStopBinds` (CRW-1405) bound, through `sessionVerify`, every running turn of the subagent that carried the marker of the step the stop came to and was not the call's bound turn, without asking whether that step was already bound to a turn (by `sessionBindStopped`, or by an earlier turn of the same stop read). A second `send_input` turn with the same `crw-flow-op: <marker>` (the step's input sent twice) made `sessionVerify` bind the step again to the newest turn carrying the marker: a second (and, as the stop read the bound turn after it, a third) `turn_started` row for the step, the call's bound turn moved to the second turn, and the turn the rows had bound first left running, so the resumed process waited on a turn nobody interrupted; port: fixed (a step bound to a turn keeps it: `sessionStopBinds` binds a running turn only while the step the stop came to is bound to no turn, `s.boundK == step.k && s.turn != ""` returns false before `sessionVerify`, so the other running turn is neither bound nor interrupted, as the stop already treats a running turn no row of the call names. The recorded positions of CRW-1384 and CRW-1405 hold: the run process stays the one writer after its started row, and no relay store format changed. Pinned by `TestSessionStopNeverBindsABoundStepAgain`, whose host starts the repair turn at the stop's bind read and a second turn with its marker at the stop's read of the running turns, over the memory and the SQLite journal: the stop interrupts the bound repair turn only, the rows hold one `turn_started` for the repair step, and after the process dies at the interrupt the resumed call is held `engine_paused` with no further `turn_started`).
