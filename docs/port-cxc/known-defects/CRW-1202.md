# CRW-1202 — the continuity of the architect and the re-plan: the design ledger, the signal path, the numbers of SP-ARCH-2

Source: CRW's own design (0.5.0-alpha, issue F09), not the CXC oracle: CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac) has the architect role (`internal/role/agents/architect.toml`, whose lines 17-20 are the continuity of its decisions) and no re-plan. The Go side is `internal/flow/ops/{replan,lane_design,finalgate,lane_close,lane,proofs}.go`, `internal/flow/engine/{signal,call,resume,backend,engine,scriptvm,opcall}.go` and `internal/flow/backend/agent/{agent,worktree}.go`; the script side is `internal/flow/workflows/{project-lane.js,lib/lane.js,lib/packets.js}`; the rule is written in `docs/flow/script.md`, "The architect's continuity and the re-plan", and in `docs/flow/journal.md`. The tests are `internal/flow/scenario/replan_test.go`, `internal/flow/engine/signal_test.go`, `internal/flow/ops/replan_test.go` and `internal/flow/backend/agent/architect_cwd_test.go`.

- Supersedes CRW-1206.md, items beginning "reviewer-final (SP-REVF-1) reads the integrated head H" (its last sentence, on what the lane does with an alignment of `MISALIGNED` or a verdict of `needs_changes`) and "Not done here: a `needs_changes` or `MISALIGNED` answer of reviewer-final": now port: fixed for the alignment (a MISALIGNED reviewer-final and a MISALIGNED REFLECTION are reasons to re-plan; a milestone that closed with a reason is `replanned`, the architect's delta is audited again and recorded as the next revision, and the orchestrator is asked for the correction issues). A `needs_changes` verdict of reviewer-final is still only recorded. port: kept for the verdict.
- Supersedes CRW-1201.md, item beginning "The design ledger holds revision `r0` only": now port: fixed (`crw.designLedger` takes the next revision, an entry names none or the next one; revision ids are used once - a revision a resume superseded keeps its id and the next is named after every row - and the revision that stands is the newest row not superseded). `crw.issuePlan` still reads the row of the revision the node's release row names, which a revision appended later does not change.
- Supersedes CRW-1201.md, item beginning "`crw.dag` is a composite on `parallel` with its own gates": now port: fixed for the signal paths (the engine opens them as the items of a join, below; `crw.dag` stays a composite). Supersedes CRW-1201.md, item beginning "`crw.dag` admits a node by the regions": the scheduler gate reads the regions of the first revision until a signal makes another: a refresh signal hands the dag the admission regions of the nodes under the revision it made (`regions` in the outcome of `onSignal`, from `crw.currentDesign`), which the gate reads for every node that has not been admitted. A node the gate admitted before the signal began and that is not yet released takes the new regions too, and is checked against the nodes that hold their regions once more before its release (it waits for the end of the lane it now overlaps); a node released before the signal keeps the regions it was admitted by. port: fixed (kept for the released nodes).
- The body says `crw.milestoneHalt`; it is a composite of `crw:lib/lane` (it records through `crw.hold` and `crw.escalate`) and not an operation, because it has no effect of its own to replay: it is the `halt` function of `project-lane.js`, moved. `crw.replanSignals` is an operation (it reads the plan and writes the two numbers). port: kept.
- The body's `onSignal` of `crw.dag` runs on a "child path `signal:<milestone>:<k>`": the signals are the items of a one-item join each, on the path `signals:<milestone>` which is an item of the dag's join next to the lanes; the child path of a signal is that item (key `signal:<milestone>:<k>`, percent-encoded in the path id). One signal path runs at a time because the path is sequential, and the triggers that arrive while it runs are taken together by the next signal. The result of the dag is the list of the lanes' results as before (the signal path's own result is not in it). port: kept.
- The signal rows are `crw.signalBind` (the input), `crw.signalDone` (the result) and `crw.signalClose` (the end of the path); the body's "signal 행" is these. The body says k is decided when the row is journaled: it is the number the script gives the bind, one more than the signals that stand. The `input_digest` of the signal path is the engine's input digest of the bind position (the triggers as (call, result digest), the result digest of the signal before it): a trigger that is not replayed, or that answers another digest, is another input. The Data of the done row names the result `outcome` (the engine adds `result`, the JSON of the answer). port: kept.
- Three builtins of the engine, `crw.callFacts`, `crw.recordedSignal` and `crw.priorRelease`, take no position and write no row: the engine alone knows whether a result was replayed from the journal of an earlier process (a result carries no such fact; one that did would give two digests to the same call), and the standing rows of the earlier processes are read from the journal. They are not in the body; the body's "재개 때 membership과 revision은 행에서 재생하고 live 시점에서 다시 계산하지 않는다" needs them. port: kept.
- A trigger is the RECEIPT of a completed executor call of a lane (`sig.receipt(result)`, the third argument of the lane function) or a plan revision a lane read (`sig.plan(read)`). The dag has no feed of plan revisions of its own: the relay seam is read where the lane asks it for its release, so a lane reads the plan with `crw.planRead(milestone, node)` before `crw.release`, and the read is a trigger when the plan digest is another than the one the last signal read (or the first revision was made against): kind `revision`, call `plan:<digest>`, the digest, once for the digest, the journal position of the read for its order. The lane then waits (`admit`) for the signal that comes of it, so a plan revision that pushes the slice change over a quarter is re-planned before the nodes that have not started are released, with no RECEIPT in between. The node whose release read the change is held too (the read is before its release). A plan that changes with no lane asking for a release is noticed at the next release or RECEIPT: the run has no other place to read the relay. A replayed read replays what it recorded. A read stands when the node was released after it, or when a signal row that stands took the changed revision it read as a trigger: read again after a crash between the signal and the release, it would take that signal's plan digest as its base, answer no change, and the signal it was made of could not replay (the re-plan turn would run again and the node would be released under the revision before it). Any other read the node was not released after is made again, so a plan that changed while no process ran is seen. The lane waits until the signal that took its revision trigger is decided whether that signal runs live or is replayed. port: fixed.
- The number of plan mismatches counts the RECEIPTs that the signals so far took in, not every RECEIPT of the journal: a RECEIPT replayed by a resume and a RECEIPT that ran again are counted by the signal that took them in, and a RECEIPT of a call a resume superseded is not. The body says "서로 다른 RECEIPT 수"; a call is counted once. A lane that keeps its replay under a revision that stands (`release_rebind`) counts under that revision. port: kept.
- A recorded signal whose trigger ran again is bound with the argument `attempt` = `live:<n>`, `n` one more than the attempt of the row it does not replay (`""` counts as 0, and `live`, the attempt of a row bound before attempts were numbered, as 1), so the position runs again even when the trigger answers the digest it answered - also on a second resume that runs a trigger again after a resume that already did, whose recorded row is itself a live attempt (scenario `TestSecondLiveTriggerResumeSupersedesTheRevisionOfTheFirst`: the third process binds signal 1 as `live:2`, supersedes the second process's signal rows and ledger row and writes `revision_superseded` for its revision, and a fourth process with nothing changed is zero-live; with the one constant `live` the third process replayed the second's bind, re-plan and revision and released B under it); `crw.signalBind` refuses any other attempt (`TestSignalBindAttempts`). The row records the attempt. A later resume with nothing changed replays that row: `crw.recordedSignal` returns the row's `attempt` and the signal path hands the same value to `crw.signalBind`, so the arguments, and with them the input digest of the position, are the recorded ones and no signal, close, final review or re-plan runs live (scenario `TestUnchangedResumeAfterALiveTriggerResumeIsZeroLive`; without the attempt in the replay the third process re-ran every signal and re-planned a closed milestone). port: kept.
- A release records the slice digest of its node as the relay reads the plan when the node is released (`slice_digest`), where F08 read the milestone row the run took first; the criteria digest and the criteria in the packet are still those of the plan the run read first. Without it a node's slice could never differ from the one the revision that stands was made against, and the body's comparison would always be equal. The slice of a node is compared with `slices` of the ledger row (the milestone row for r0; for a revision after it the plan the architect and the auditor evaluated, which `crw.replanSignals` names as `plan` and the script hands `crw.designLedger`, so a plan revision made while the delta was audited is counted by the next signal and not absorbed into the baseline). The release reads the plan again and releases nothing when it cannot (an error) or when the plan has dropped the node (`node_not_in_plan`): no slice of the plan the run read first stands in for the live one. port: kept.
- A lane that keeps its replay (`release_rebind`) goes on under the DESIGN text of the revision it was built from, which is the superseded one; "재생을 유지" is read as keeping the calls the lane made, and the packet of a call is part of its input digest. A node whose slice is another is released again under the revision that stands, so its packet is that revision's. A node a landed acceptance rests on, whose slice is another, is refused by `crw.release` with `accepted_node_changed` and the route (`update_node` or `dag-correct`), as the engine refuses an accepted node whose spec changed. port: kept.
- No new release while a signal is decided: a lane that has not released waits (`crw:lib/lane`, `admit`); a lane that has released goes on. A lane that a resume finds released under a revision a signal made waits until that signal is decided. While the signals an earlier process recorded are not yet decided, the scheduler admits only the nodes they concern (a lane that was released, the lanes it waits for, the lanes a recorded trigger names); a node released for the first time in that window would stand under a revision that is about to be superseded and would land on its DESIGN with no rebind. A signal an earlier process recorded and that does not replay is bound with the argument `attempt` `live:<n>` (`crw.signalBind`, `n` one more than the recorded row's), because its triggers can answer the digests they answered, byte for byte, when their lane ran a call live (a changed prompt, an effect proof that no longer holds): the engine compares only arguments, and the signal, the re-plan and the revision it made must not replay then. A trigger whose lane has run a call live is settled at once, so the live signal that takes the triggers over does not wait for the end of that lane (a recorded signal made of two RECEIPTs of one lane, the second of which the resume does not make). No recorded signal is looked up after the first one that did not replay; the engine has already superseded their rows then, so this only states the rule. The scheduler also admits no node while a signal is decided. The body does not say how the stop is made; this is not a barrier on the running lanes. A re-plan that is due when no node is left to release is skipped (`no_unstarted_node`) and the close counts the same numbers. port: kept.
- The REFLECTION runs at every close (after `crw.closeMilestone`), not only when reviewer-final is MISALIGNED, because the body makes a MISALIGNED REFLECTION a reason of its own; the scenarios of CRW-1201 and CRW-1206 gain the call `reflection:M1` after `reviewer-final:M1`. A milestone that closed with a reason is `replanned` and runs neither `crw.settleReviews` nor `crw.reportUp`: the correction issues land first. A `reviewer-final` that is MISALIGNED is not a halt of the close: the close is recorded, as F13 does. port: kept.
- The head of an architect turn: a continuation of the architect that names `head` gets a call-private detached worktree at that head, `<state>/flow/<run>/calls/<call_id>/wt` (a later allocation of the same call `wt-1`, ...), named as the turn's cwd, and removed when the call has its terminal row (`engine.DirRemover`, `Backend.RemoveDir`); a call that goes on in place or runs again makes it again at the head of its `prepared` row. A continuation with no head, the fix of a DESIGN included, keeps the directory its thread started in, which the turn also names. The body does not name `engine/` for this; the files `internal/flow/engine/{call,backend}.go` change by this only. port: kept.
- A standing signal of the milestone keeps every changed plan read whose `plan:<digest>` it took as a trigger, regardless of the order of their terminal rows. Concurrent lanes can finish reads of the same revision after the signal bind; requiring the bind's sequence to be greater superseded those reads on an unchanged resume. `TestPlanReadProofDoesNotDependOnSignalBindOrder` covers both orders, another milestone or revision, an unchanged read and a superseded bind; `TestResumeDuringPlanRevisionSignalZeroLive` exercises the concurrent crash/resume path. port: fixed.
- Not done here: the delivery of the report (F11), the real relay seam of the plan's revisions, and the count of how often a real milestone re-plans (F17).
- Observed while testing, not changed: a process of the scenario harness ended at a row while another lane has a `thread/start` in flight leaves a creation that the next resume has to look for with `thread/list`; under a loaded host that pass sometimes waits a long time and the run ends with the step limit. The scenarios that end a process at a row run one lane at a time (`Lab.Cap`).
