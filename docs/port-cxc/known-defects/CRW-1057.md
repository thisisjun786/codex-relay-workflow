# CRW-1057 — relay service start passes the caller's descriptors to the daemon

Source: Linear CRW-1057 (observed 10-09 09:08 KST on the 885 runtime installation: the installer's integration lock on fd 9 was inherited by the relay supervisor (pid 2344412) and its worker (pid 2344426)). The oracle is CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac), which is not in this clone, so its launch lines are not cited. The Go files are internal/relay/service/launch.go (launch, which marks the descriptors before cmd.Start), internal/relay/service/inherit.go (closeOnExecInherited, with inherit_linux.go and inherit_other.go for close_range), the sweep tests internal/relay/service/inherit_sweep_linux_test.go, inherit_refuse_linux_test.go and inherit_test.go and the red test internal/relay/service/inherit_linux_test.go (TestServiceStartDoesNotInheritCallerDescriptors). The red and green runs are /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/red-2-before-fix.log and green-1-after-fix.log.

- `relay service start` (Start, Restart, and the supervisor and worker spawns through launch) gave the supervisor and the worker every descriptor above 2 that the caller held without close-on-exec: os/exec forks those into the child, and only ExtraFiles are placed on purpose. A shell's `exec 9>lock` therefore stayed held by the long-lived daemon. The oracle's launch goes through subprocess.Popen (the NUL refusal in launch.go names it), whose default close_fds=True closes them. port: fixed (launch marks every descriptor above 2 close-on-exec before cmd.Start, so the fork passes on only the ExtraFiles it sets up; the same sweep stops the heavy-slot descriptors 8 and 9 of tools/heavy_slot.sh from reaching a daemon that a test starts).
- A background job (`relay job run`, internal/relay/dispatch/job.go -> internal/relay/job/verbs.go RunBackground -> spawn.go, exec.Command with Setsid and no ExtraFiles) gives its child the descriptors of the job CLI process, which are its caller's. That is the same class of leak on a separate path, and it is not part of a service start: the relay worker's loop never calls RunBackground (the only importers of internal/relay/job outside tests are the job CLI and the cmd/crw hooks), and the worker's own non-close-on-exec descriptors (the daemon lock and the scope lock, which the worker adopts from the supervisor's ExtraFiles) are not reachable from a job. The startup publication pipe is read to its end and closed by PublishWorkerPolicy before the worker's loop starts (internal/relay/service/publication.go, internal/relay/cli/daemon.go), so it is not held by anything the worker later spawns. Noted by reading the code, not reproduced here; port: pending (a follow-up of its own, not this issue).
- The sweep does not depend on one path being mounted: an isolated Linux (bwrap with /proc but only null, zero, random and urandom under /dev) has no /dev/fd, and the first version, which listed /dev/fd and returned its error, then refused every service start (verification of f938745f0, red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix1/red-nodevfd-before-fix.log). closeOnExecInherited now tries close_range(3, max, CLOSE_RANGE_CLOEXEC), then /proc/self/fd, then /dev/fd, then walks the descriptor numbers, and never fails the start. port: fixed (green /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix1/green-nodevfd-after-fix.log).
- The last-resort walk first stopped at the soft RLIMIT_NOFILE, but lowering the limit does not close descriptors already open above it, so a caller that lowered the limit after opening fd 1000 (or exec'd the start with it held) kept fd 1000 in the daemon when close_range and both listings were unavailable (verification of 794b8188, red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix2/red-lowered-limit-before-fix.log, TestFDSweepWalksPastLoweredLimits for a lowered soft limit and a lowered hard limit). The walk covers every number an open descriptor can have: the larger of the soft limit, the hard limit and the kernel's maximum (fs.nr_open, which no descriptor reaches; read from /proc/sys/fs/nr_open), up to 1<<24 numbers (about a second). port: fixed (green /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix2/green-lowered-limit-after-fix.log).
- Re-evaluation of 9c0015af (d1, d2), the oracle behaves like the findings: CPython's subprocess close_fds=True (the oracle's Popen) ignores a failed close in the child and, without close_range and without /proc/self/fd, closes only up to sysconf(SC_OPEN_MAX) (the soft limit), so a descriptor it cannot close, or one above the soft limit, goes on to the child. Both are real defects of the in-promise behaviour (the caller's lock reaching the daemon), so the Go service deviates on purpose. port: deviation, fixed. (d1) A descriptor that cannot be marked close-on-exec (a sandbox refusing close_range and fcntl(F_SETFD) with EPERM, while open, dup3 and /proc/self/fd work) used to be dropped silently, because unix.CloseOnExec returns no error and a readable listing counted as success; markCloseOnExec now checks F_GETFD, F_SETFD and F_GETFD again, and every way of the sweep returns the first failure, so launch refuses the start with the daemon not started (red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix3/red-before-fix.log, TestFDSweepRefusesWhatItCannotMark with a real seccomp filter, and TestLaunchRefusedWhenTheSweepFails; green /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix3/green-after-fix.log). (d2) The last-resort walk used to stop at max(soft, hard, 1<<20), which is not an upper bound once the limits were lowered after a descriptor was opened above them (for example 1500000 on a host whose fs.nr_open and limits are 2097152 or more). The bound is now the kernel's maximum, and when it is unknown (no /proc/sys/fs/nr_open) or above 1<<24 the sweep fails with ErrDescriptorsUnbounded and the start is refused instead of leaking; this host's fs.nr_open is 2147483584, so there the last resort refuses (TestFDSweepLastResortCoversEveryNumberOrRefuses; TestDescriptorBound; TestFDSweepWalksPastLoweredLimits holds a descriptor at 90000 under limits lowered to 64 with a kernel maximum of 100000). The consequence: a host with no close_range, no /proc/self/fd and no /dev/fd, and a kernel maximum above 1<<24 or unreadable, cannot start the service; one of those listings or close_range is available on every Linux this runs on (kernel 5.11 and up, or a mounted /proc), and macOS lists /dev/fd.
