# CRW-1057 — relay service start passes the caller's descriptors to the daemon

Source: Linear CRW-1057 (observed 10-09 09:08 KST on the 885 runtime installation: the installer's integration lock on fd 9 was inherited by the relay supervisor (pid 2344412) and its worker (pid 2344426)). The oracle is CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac), which is not in this clone, so its launch lines are not cited. The Go files are internal/relay/service/launch.go (launch, which marks the descriptors before cmd.Start), internal/relay/service/inherit.go (closeOnExecInherited, which calls the shared sweep fdsweep.MarkInherited in internal/relay/fdsweep/sweep.go with closerange_linux.go and closerange_other.go for close_range; the sweep moved there in CRW-1081), the sweep tests internal/relay/fdsweep/sweep_test.go, sweep_linux_test.go and refuse_linux_test.go, the launch refusal test internal/relay/service/inherit_test.go and the red test internal/relay/service/inherit_linux_test.go (TestServiceStartDoesNotInheritCallerDescriptors). The red and green runs are /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/red-2-before-fix.log and green-1-after-fix.log.

- `relay service start` (Start, Restart, and the supervisor and worker spawns through launch) gave the supervisor and the worker every descriptor above 2 that the caller held without close-on-exec: os/exec forks those into the child, and only ExtraFiles are placed on purpose. A shell's `exec 9>lock` therefore stayed held by the long-lived daemon. The oracle's launch goes through subprocess.Popen (the NUL refusal in launch.go names it), whose default close_fds=True closes them. port: fixed (launch marks every descriptor above 2 close-on-exec before cmd.Start, so the fork passes on only the ExtraFiles it sets up; the same sweep stops the heavy-slot descriptors 8 and 9 of tools/heavy_slot.sh from reaching a daemon that a test starts).
- A background job (`relay job run`, internal/relay/dispatch/job.go -> internal/relay/job/verbs.go RunBackground -> spawn.go, exec.Command with Setsid and no ExtraFiles) gives its child the descriptors of the job CLI process, which are its caller's. That is the same class of leak on a separate path, and it is not part of a service start: the relay worker's loop never calls RunBackground (the only importers of internal/relay/job outside tests are the job CLI and the cmd/crw hooks), and the worker's own non-close-on-exec descriptors (the daemon lock and the scope lock, which the worker adopts from the supervisor's ExtraFiles) are not reachable from a job. The startup publication pipe is read to its end and closed by PublishWorkerPolicy before the worker's loop starts (internal/relay/service/publication.go, internal/relay/cli/daemon.go), so it is not held by anything the worker later spawns. Noted by reading the code, not reproduced here; port: fixed in CRW-1081 (see CRW-1081.md).
- How the sweep reaches the descriptors: the sweep (fdsweep.MarkInherited) tries close_range(3, ~0U, CLOSE_RANGE_CLOEXEC), which covers every descriptor number, then a listing of the open descriptors in /proc/self/fd, then in /dev/fd, and the first way that is available decides. A missing path alone does not fail the start, because the next way is tried: an isolated Linux (bwrap with /proc but only null, zero, random and urandom under /dev) has no /dev/fd, and a sweep that required it refused every service start there (verification of f938745f0, red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix1/red-nodevfd-before-fix.log, green fix1/green-nodevfd-after-fix.log). port: fixed.
- The start is refused (launch returns the error and the daemon is not started) when the sweep cannot show that every caller descriptor is kept out of the daemon. The oracle's subprocess close_fds=True (CPython) ignores a failed close in the child and, without close_range and without /proc/self/fd, closes only up to sysconf(SC_OPEN_MAX), the soft limit, so a descriptor it cannot close, or one above the soft limit, goes on to the child. Both are the in-promise defect (the caller's lock reaching the daemon), so the Go service deviates on purpose. port: deviation, fixed. (1) A descriptor that cannot be marked close-on-exec: markCloseOnExec checks F_GETFD, F_SETFD and F_GETFD again, and the first failure fails the sweep (re-evaluation of 9c0015af, d1; red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix3/red-before-fix.log, TestFDSweepRefusesWhatItCannotMark with a real seccomp filter that fails close_range and fcntl(F_SETFD), and TestLaunchRefusedWhenTheSweepFails; green fix3/green-after-fix.log). (2) No way to reach every descriptor: with close_range not offered and neither listing readable the sweep fails with ErrDescriptorsUnlisted. No number bounds the descriptors already open, because lowering RLIMIT_NOFILE or fs.nr_open closes nothing, so a walk of the descriptor numbers up to such a bound is no proof and there is none: a walk to the soft limit left a descriptor opened before the limit was lowered (red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix2/red-lowered-limit-before-fix.log), and a walk to the larger of both limits and fs.nr_open left one opened before fs.nr_open was lowered (verification of feaf05dc; red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix4/red-p1a-before-fix.log, TestFDSweepPastLoweredLimitsMarksOrRefuses; green fix4/green-after-fix.log). The consequence: a host where close_range with CLOSE_RANGE_CLOEXEC is unavailable (Linux before 5.11, or a sandbox that blocks it) and neither /proc/self/fd nor /dev/fd can be read cannot start the service; Linux with /proc mounted, and macOS (which lists /dev/fd), always have a way.
