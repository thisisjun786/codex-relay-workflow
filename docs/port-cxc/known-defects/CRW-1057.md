# CRW-1057 — relay service start passes the caller's descriptors to the daemon

Source: Linear CRW-1057 (observed 10-09 09:08 KST on the 885 runtime installation: the installer's integration lock on fd 9 was inherited by the relay supervisor (pid 2344412) and its worker (pid 2344426)). The oracle is CXC v0.2.40 (commit 3c1459ac), which is not in this clone, so its launch lines are not cited. The Go files are internal/relay/service/launch.go (launch, which marks the descriptors before cmd.Start), internal/relay/service/inherit.go (closeOnExecInherited, with inherit_linux.go and inherit_other.go for close_range), the sweep tests internal/relay/service/inherit_sweep_linux_test.go and the red test internal/relay/service/inherit_linux_test.go (TestServiceStartDoesNotInheritCallerDescriptors). The red and green runs are /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/red-2-before-fix.log and green-1-after-fix.log.

- `relay service start` (Start, Restart, and the supervisor and worker spawns through launch) gave the supervisor and the worker every descriptor above 2 that the caller held without close-on-exec: os/exec forks those into the child, and only ExtraFiles are placed on purpose. A shell's `exec 9>lock` therefore stayed held by the long-lived daemon. The oracle's launch goes through subprocess.Popen (the NUL refusal in launch.go names it), whose default close_fds=True closes them. port: fixed (launch marks every descriptor above 2 close-on-exec before cmd.Start, so the fork passes on only the ExtraFiles it sets up; the same sweep stops the heavy-slot descriptors 8 and 9 of tools/heavy_slot.sh from reaching a daemon that a test starts).
- A background job started by the worker (internal/relay/job/spawn.go, exec.Command with Setsid and no ExtraFiles) still gives its child the worker's own non-close-on-exec descriptors at 3 to 5 (the daemon lock, the scope lock and the publication copy). Noted by reading the code, not reproduced here; port: pending.
- The sweep does not depend on one path being mounted: an isolated Linux (bwrap with /proc but only null, zero, random and urandom under /dev) has no /dev/fd, and the first version, which listed /dev/fd and returned its error, then refused every service start (verification of f938745f0, red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix1/red-nodevfd-before-fix.log). closeOnExecInherited now tries close_range(3, max, CLOSE_RANGE_CLOEXEC), then /proc/self/fd, then /dev/fd, then walks the descriptor numbers, and never fails the start. port: fixed (green /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix1/green-nodevfd-after-fix.log).
- The last-resort walk first stopped at the soft RLIMIT_NOFILE, but lowering the limit does not close descriptors already open above it, so a caller that lowered the limit after opening fd 1000 (or exec'd the start with it held) kept fd 1000 in the daemon when close_range and both listings were unavailable (verification of 794b8188, red /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix2/red-lowered-limit-before-fix.log, TestFDSweepWalksPastLoweredLimits for a lowered soft limit and a lowered hard limit). The walk now covers the larger of the soft limit, the hard limit and 1<<20 (the kernel's default fs.nr_open), up to 1<<24 descriptor numbers (about a second). port: fixed (green /state/crw/crw-1057/evidence/fix2/green-lowered-limit-after-fix.log). Residual, only on that last-resort path (kernel without close_range CLOSE_RANGE_CLOEXEC and with /proc/self/fd and /dev/fd both hidden): a descriptor numbered at or above both limits and 1<<20, or at or above 1<<24, is not reached; such a number needs root to have raised fs.nr_open and the limits first.
