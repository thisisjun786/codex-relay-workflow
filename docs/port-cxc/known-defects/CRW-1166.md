# CRW-1166 — the loop-arm scope word heuristic keeps reading an object child, a Korean connective and a bare worker subject wrongly

Source: follows CRW-1084 (`CRW-1084.md`), whose scope classifier has no CXC counterpart: the oracle (CXC v0.2.40 commit 3c1459ac, `plugins/codexclaw/components/pabcd-state/src/hook.ts:700-746`) gave every un-negated loop request the implementation recipe. The Go file is `internal/pabcd/hook/detect.go` (`ClassifyLoopArmScope`, `delegation`, `hangulConnective`, `coordinateKorean`, `implementVerbs`); the cases are in `internal/pabcd/hook/prompt_loop_scope_crw1084_test.go` (`TestClassifyLoopArmScope`, `TestLoopArmScopeCases`). Every row that existed before keeps its result.

- A child noun that is the object of a transitive verb and is followed only by sentence adverbs read as the verb's subject (port: fixed): "Use crw-loop in this session to consult the children then implement this task." and "... consult the workers then implement this task in the migration project." and "consult the children, then, implement this task" got the project pointer although the request is a current-task implementation. `delegation` now looks at the structure before the noun: a transitive verb (consult, ask, read, check, inspect, review, tell, "check with", "wait for", ...) right before it, only commas and then/also/next/afterwards after it, makes the noun an object and the session the implementer. A subordinating conjunction or a causative before the noun ("while/as/when the children then implement", "let the children then implement") leaves it the subject, so those stay a coordination. Pinned by the CRW-1166 rows of both tables.
- A named agent that is the subject of an implement verb but is no coordination word read as nothing (port: fixed): "Use crw-loop in this session while the workers meanwhile implement their issues." got `LoopScopeNone` and the recipe. A delegated implement verb (`implementVerbs`) whose agent is a worker, subagent, child task or the Korean 자식/하위/워커 now counts as a coordination (`delegatedImplement`). The pronouns "they" and "others" carry no such signal (they are too often a plain pronoun: "..., they said fix the lint" stays `LoopScopeNone`).
- A Korean 조정 counted as coordination wherever it stood (port: fixed, a CRW-1084 heuristic): "crw-loop로 타임아웃 값 조정해줘" got the project pointer. `coordinateKorean` reads 조정 as an adjustment, not a coordination, when a setting-like noun (값, 설정, 간격, 크기, 폰트, 좌표, ...) comes right before it (optionally with a particle or the prefix 재) or 값/폭/량/치 right after it. Any other 조정 ("레인들을 조정", "작업 순서 조정") stays a coordination: the list is closed and the default is the pointer.
- A Korean child noun followed by the connective -고 read as the verb's subject (port: fixed): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식 작업을 확인하고 구현해" got the pointer. `hangulConnective` treats a 자식/하위/워커 noun as the object of the earlier verb when a word ending in 고 follows it within the three-word window and neither the noun nor a word before that connective ends in a subject particle (이/가/은/는 after another character). "자식이 확인하고 구현해", "자식 작업이 확인하고 구현해" and "자식에게 구현하게 해" stay delegated.
- The -고 exception only holds while nothing else owns the verb (port: fixed, verification round 1): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식에게 확인하고 구현하게 해" and "... 자식 작업을 확인하고 워커가 구현해" got the recipe and `loopArmSeen` although a delegate or an explicit worker subject implements. `hangulConnective` now also stays delegated when a causative follows the verb (구현하게/하도록/하라고/시켜) or when a word after the connective is another agent noun or ends in a subject particle (이/가/은/는) that is not first-person ("제가 구현해" stays this session).
- Remaining ambiguity (not fixed, safe direction): an object whose noun is further than three words from the verb, a pronoun object ("ask them to implement"), the verbs outside the closed `objectLead` list and a Korean subject particle on a word that merely ends in 이/가/은/는 keep the project pointer, which sets no `loopArmSeen`.
