# CRW-1166 — the loop-arm scope word heuristic keeps reading an object child, a Korean connective and a bare worker subject wrongly

Source: follows CRW-1084 (`CRW-1084.md`), whose scope classifier has no CXC counterpart: the oracle (CXC v0.2.40 commit 3c1459ac, `plugins/codexclaw/components/pabcd-state/src/hook.ts:700-746`) gave every un-negated loop request the implementation recipe. The Go file is `internal/pabcd/hook/detect.go` (`ClassifyLoopArmScope`, `delegation`, `hangulConnective`, `coordinateKorean`, `implementVerbs`); the cases are in `internal/pabcd/hook/prompt_loop_scope_crw1084_test.go` (`TestClassifyLoopArmScope`, `TestLoopArmScopeCases`). Every row that existed before keeps its result.

- A child noun that is the object of a transitive verb and is followed only by sentence adverbs read as the verb's subject (port: fixed): "Use crw-loop in this session to consult the children then implement this task." and "... consult the workers then implement this task in the migration project." and "consult the children, then, implement this task" got the project pointer although the request is a current-task implementation. `delegation` now looks at the structure before the noun: a transitive verb (consult, ask, read, check, inspect, review, tell, "check with", "wait for", ...) right before it, only commas and then/also/next/afterwards after it, makes the noun an object and the session the implementer. A subordinating conjunction or a causative before the noun ("while/as/when the children then implement", "let the children then implement") leaves it the subject, so those stay a coordination. Pinned by the CRW-1166 rows of both tables.
- A named agent that is the subject of an implement verb but is no coordination word read as nothing (port: fixed): "Use crw-loop in this session while the workers meanwhile implement their issues." got `LoopScopeNone` and the recipe. A delegated implement verb (`implementVerbs`) whose agent is a worker, subagent, child task or the Korean 자식/하위/워커 now counts as a coordination (`delegatedImplement`). The pronouns "they" and "others" carry no such signal (they are too often a plain pronoun: "..., they said fix the lint" stays `LoopScopeNone`).
- A Korean 조정 counted as coordination wherever it stood (port: fixed, a CRW-1084 heuristic): "crw-loop로 타임아웃 값 조정해줘" got the project pointer. `coordinateKorean` reads 조정 as an adjustment, not a coordination, when a setting-like noun (값, 설정, 간격, 크기, 폰트, 좌표, ...) comes right before it (optionally with a particle or the prefix 재) or 값/폭/량/치 right after it. Any other 조정 ("레인들을 조정", "작업 순서 조정") stays a coordination: the list is closed and the default is the pointer.
- A Korean child noun followed by the connective -고 read as the verb's subject (port: fixed): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식 작업을 확인하고 구현해" got the pointer. `hangulConnective` treats a 자식/하위/워커 noun as the object of the earlier verb when a word ending in 고 follows it within the three-word window and neither the noun nor a word before that connective ends in a subject particle (이/가/은/는 after another character). "자식이 확인하고 구현해", "자식 작업이 확인하고 구현해" and "자식에게 구현하게 해" stay delegated.
- The -고 exception only holds while nothing else owns the verb (port: fixed, verification round 1): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식에게 확인하고 구현하게 해" and "... 자식 작업을 확인하고 워커가 구현해" got the recipe and `loopArmSeen` although a delegate or an explicit worker subject implements. `hangulConnective` now also stays delegated when a causative follows the verb (구현하게/하도록/하라고/시켜) or when a word after the connective is another agent noun or ends in a subject particle (이/가/은/는) that is not first-person ("제가 구현해" stays this session).
- A causative at the end of a -고 chain governs every implement verb of the chain (port: fixed, verification round 2): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식에게 확인하고 구현하고 수정하게 해" and "... 자식 작업을 확인하고 구현하고 수정하도록 해" got the recipe and `loopArmSeen` because only the word right after 구현 was checked for a causative. `chainCausative` follows the chain of connectives (-고, -서, -며) after the verb and stays delegated when a later verb carries a causative (-게 해, -도록, -라고, 시켜/시키); a chain that ends without one ("구현하고 수정해", "제가 구현하고 수정할게") stays this session's. Pinned by the CRW-1166 rows of both tables.
- An object or a bare noun between the verbs of a -고 chain no longer hides the causative (port: fixed, evaluation d1): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식에게 확인하고 구현하고 테스트를 수정하게 해" and "... 구현하고 테스트 수정하도록 해" got the recipe and `loopArmSeen` because `chainCausative` stopped at the first word that was no connective. It now skips such words and stays delegated at a causative later in the same sentence. The chain ends, and the verb stays this session's, at a finite verb without a causative ("구현하고 테스트를 수정해"), at a sentence end and at another agent noun ("구현하고 워커에게 테스트하게 해": that agent's own verb).
- An implement-keyword noun beside an agent noun is no delegated verb (port: fixed, evaluation d2, a regression of the new `delegatedImplement` project signal): "Use crw-loop to optimize the worker build cache for this task." got the project pointer; dev (and the oracle) gave the recipe. `nounUse` keeps `delegatedImplement` from firing when a singular English agent noun (worker, subagent, child task, ...) stands right before the bare keyword (a compound modifier: a singular subject takes "builds" or "will build") or when the Korean 구현/수정/개발 has no 하/해/시 ending ("워커 개발 환경"). Plural nouns ("the workers build their parts") and "workers meanwhile implement" keep the coordination signal. The session decision is untouched.
- A causative before a singular agent noun keeps its bare verb delegated (port: fixed, verification round 3, a regression of the `nounUse` compound rule): "Use crw-loop to have the worker build its assigned part." and "Use crw-loop and let the subagent implement its assigned issue." got `LoopScopeNone` and the recipe because every bare keyword right after a singular agent noun was read as a compound. `nounUse` now leaves the keyword a verb when have, let, make, get or help (any form, optionally with a determiner) stands right before the noun; an ambiguous "have the worker build cache rebuilt" keeps the pointer too. "optimize the worker build cache" stays a compound.
- A noun or a postposition that merely ends like a finite verb no longer ends a -고 chain (port: fixed, verification round 3): "crw-loop 돌려서 현재 세션에서 자식에게 확인하고 구현하고 개요 수정하도록 해" got the recipe and `loopArmSeen` because the one-syllable finite-ending test took 개요 for a finite verb. `finiteKorean` reads 해, 줘, 죠 and only those 요/다/라/래/게 endings whose syllable before makes them a verb ending (해요, 고쳐요, 합니다, 한다, 해라, 할래, 할게); 개요, 필요, 바다, 미래, 무게 and the postpositions 에 대해, 위해, 통해 do not end the chain. An ending it does not read keeps the search going, which errs to the pointer.
- Remaining ambiguity (not fixed): an agent noun further than three words from the verb ("Use crw-loop in this session to ask the workers to carefully and independently implement their issues.") - also a later implement verb of a Korean -고 chain whose own causative stands more than three words after the agent noun ("자식에게 확인하고 구현하고 버그에 대해 수정하게 해": 수정 is read as this session's own, although the chain reading of 구현 is delegated), a pronoun object ("ask them to implement"), the verbs outside the closed `objectLead` list and a Korean subject particle on a word that merely ends in 이/가/은/는 are not recognised as another agent's verb. An unmatched agent leaves the verb this session's own, so such a request with a this/current-session mention takes the implementation recipe and sets `loopArmSeen` (CurrentTask bypasses the registered-role read) - the behaviour of dev and of the oracle, which gave every un-negated loop request the recipe. Only a recognised structure (object lead, Korean -고 connective, singular compound) moves a request away from that default.
