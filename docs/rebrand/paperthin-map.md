## Follow-up ownership

CRW-1537 owns intake and the evidence needed to understand it; CRW-1538 owns design; CRW-1539 owns critique and crosscheck; CRW-1540 owns the immediate post-implementation check and document consistency; CRW-1541 owns writing polish; CRW-1542 owns PR, merge and retrospective handoffs. The row assignments above are the detailed allocation. The excluded upgrade workflow has no implementation follow-up. When a target has several owners, each follow-up edits only the clause that implements its assigned row and preserves the shared rule's existing owner.

## CRW contracts retained by the adaptations

These are constraints on the planned follow-ups, not changes made here:

- User scope, repository policy and CRW's role settings remain authoritative. A principle never grants a new model selection, helper spawn, tracking write, release or merge permission. Upstream user-only skills do not become unconditional checks; only the bounded principle stated in the row is proposed.
- The critic reviews the design before construction. Unit check and crosscheck are tool executions, not new reviewer agents. The unit-check Codex review profile sends no free-form prompt; writing a skill or role instruction does not prove that tool consumed it. CRW-1540 must distinguish the executor's post-edit check from the shipped tool; CRW-1539's crosscheck text has its actual home in `internal/flow/workflows/lib/packets.js`.
- Existing required tests, review schemas, severity rules and verifier receipts keep their meaning. A cold read, agreement among reviewers or a path-existence test proves neither runtime enforcement nor live success.
- Tool-specific evidence and attribution stay exact. No adaptation removes license notices, contributor credit, failed-case evidence or the distinction between proposed, implemented, verified, integrated and released work.
